<Definition term={"Foundation Models and LLMs"}>
Foundation models are large pre-trained models that learn broad representations from large-scale data and are adapted to many downstream tasks through prompting, retrieval, tool use, fine-tuning or system integration. Large language models are foundation models specialised for text and code; production use requires comparing capability, latency, context length, modality support, cost, safety behaviour, data policy and operational reliability.
</Definition>

## Why It Matters

A foundation model is trained broadly enough to be adapted to many tasks. Adaptation can occur through prompting, tool use, retrieval, fine-tuning or specialised wrappers.

A large language model processes and generates sequences of tokens. Practical understanding includes tokenisation, embeddings, attention, context windows, inference, sampling, structured output, reasoning effort, caching, tool calling and model limitations.

Multimodal models process or generate more than one modality, such as text, images, audio or video. The engineer must decide how modalities are represented, combined, validated and evaluated.

Model selection is a system decision based on capability, reliability, latency, cost, privacy, context size, modality and deployment constraints. The largest model is not automatically the best production choice.

<EngineeringMode>
Foundation models are platform dependencies. Architecture must isolate model providers behind interfaces, manage context windows, rate limits, fallback models and data boundaries. Design must decide where generative behavior helps users and where deterministic flows remain safer. Build work includes prompting, retrieval, evals and guardrails. Operations manage cost, latency and vendor change. The risk is building the product around one model's current quirks.
</EngineeringMode>

<RealExample>
GPT-4, Claude and Gemini are foundation-model examples because one pretrained model can support many tasks: chat, summarization, coding, extraction, tutoring and multimodal analysis. The product work happens around the model through prompting, retrieval, safety policy, latency control, evaluation and workflow integration.
</RealExample>
