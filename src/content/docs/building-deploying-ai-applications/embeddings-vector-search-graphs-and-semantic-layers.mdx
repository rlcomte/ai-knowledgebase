<Definition term={"Embeddings, Vector Search, Graphs and Semantic Layers"}>
Embeddings are dense numeric representations that place semantically related items near each other in vector space. Vector search uses those representations for approximate semantic retrieval, while graphs and semantic layers encode entities, relationships, business meaning and constraints that pure vector similarity may miss. Robust AI systems often combine these techniques with metadata filters, ranking, provenance and evaluation.
</Definition>

## Why It Matters

Embeddings represent content as numerical vectors; vector search retrieves items with similar representations. It is useful for semantic similarity, but exact search, filters, SQL, graphs or hybrid retrieval may be better for other query types.

Knowledge graphs and semantic layers complement vector retrieval. A knowledge graph makes entities and relationships explicit. A semantic layer gives consistent business meaning to structured data and metrics.

<EngineeringMode>
These techniques imply a retrieval architecture, not just a model feature. Engineers must design source pipelines, chunking, metadata, vector indexes, graph relationships and permission filters. Product design should expose uncertainty when matches are weak. Build work covers freshness, deduplication and ranking tests. Operations monitor recall, stale indexes and access leaks. The risk is treating semantic similarity as truth rather than a candidate-selection mechanism.
</EngineeringMode>

<RealExample>
Spotify and YouTube recommendations illustrate the core idea behind embeddings: items and users can be represented by learned similarity signals. In enterprise search, the same pattern lets a question about “refund after cancelled flight” retrieve policy passages even when the document uses different wording.
</RealExample>
