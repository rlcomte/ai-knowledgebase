<Definition term={"Menselijk toezicht en guardrails"}>
Human oversight and guardrails are technical and organisational controls that limit what an AI system may do, detect unsafe or low-confidence behaviour and route consequential decisions to accountable people. Effective guardrails combine input/output validation, policy checks, tool permissions, escalation paths, audit logs and clear human decision rights.
</Definition>

## Waarom dit ertoe doet

A guardrail constrains inputs, outputs or actions. Guardrails may be deterministic rules, classifiers, policy checks, permission gates or human review. They complement rather than replace system design and evaluation.

Human in/on the loop describes meaningful human authority. In the loop usually means approval or contribution is required; on the loop means people supervise and can intervene. The control must match consequence and reversibility.

<EngineeringMode>
Oversight and guardrails must be designed into the workflow, not added as a final filter. Architecture needs policy checks, escalation paths, audit records and reversible actions. Design must show users when the system is uncertain and who owns decisions. Build work includes refusal logic and review queues. Operations measure bypasses, false escalations and incident patterns. The risk is claiming human oversight while making review impossible or ceremonial.
</EngineeringMode>

<RealExample>
Radiology AI illustrates oversight and guardrails well. A model may flag suspicious findings on an image, but the workflow should preserve clinician review, show relevant evidence, handle uncertainty and escalate edge cases. The guardrail is not only a filter; it is the decision boundary around medical responsibility.
</RealExample>
