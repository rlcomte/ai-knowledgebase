<Definition term={"Waarde, haalbaarheid, levensvatbaarheid en verantwoordelijkheid"}>
Value, feasibility, viability and responsibility are four decision lenses for judging whether an AI intervention deserves more investment. Value asks whether the change improves an outcome users or the organisation care about. Feasibility asks whether the data, models, integration and operations can work. Viability asks whether the economics and organisational ownership make sense. Responsibility asks whether risks, rights and harms are acceptable and governed.
</Definition>

## Waarom dit ertoe doet

Value, feasibility, viability and responsibility are four complementary tests: does it help users, can it be built, can it be sustained and should it be deployed under the applicable values and risks?

These tests keep teams from over-optimising model performance while ignoring adoption, operational cost, organisational constraints or harm.

<EngineeringMode>
This lens prevents narrow technical success from becoming product failure. Architecture must be feasible to operate, secure and integrate. Design must deliver user value without hiding risks. Build work tests the smallest path that proves viability. Operations measure cost, reliability and consequences for stakeholders. The recurring risk is treating the concept as a label rather than a decision framework for what should be built.
</EngineeringMode>

</EvaluationCheck>

<RealExample>
A bank considering an AI loan assistant must evaluate more than accuracy. The team should ask whether customers gain value, whether the model can integrate with legacy systems, whether support costs make business sense and whether fairness, explainability and appeal rights are acceptable before using the tool in credit workflows.
</RealExample>
