<Definition term={"Specifications, Quality Attributes and Acceptance Criteria"}>
Specifications describe expected system behaviour, inputs, outputs, constraints and integration contracts. Quality attributes define non-functional requirements such as accuracy, latency, privacy, reliability, explainability, accessibility, maintainability and cost. Acceptance criteria convert both into observable checks that determine whether a feature, model workflow or release is good enough to use.
</Definition>

## Why It Matters

A specification is a shared description of behaviour, data, constraints, edge cases and quality expectations. For probabilistic systems it includes examples, unacceptable outputs and escalation.

An acceptance criterion is a verifiable condition for completing a feature. Some criteria become deterministic tests; others become eval cases and thresholds.

A quality attribute is a cross-cutting property such as security, privacy, accessibility, reliability, latency, cost or maintainability. Attributes shape architecture and release decisions.
