<Definition term={"Risk and Responsible AI"}>
Risk and responsible AI combine hazard identification, impact assessment, control design and accountability for AI-supported decisions and actions. The work covers data protection, bias, misuse, security, transparency, human oversight, monitoring and incident response. It should produce concrete controls and ownership, not only ethical intent.
</Definition>

## Why It Matters

Risk assessment identifies hazards, likelihood, impact, affected groups, controls, residual risk and accountable owners. Risk should influence autonomy and human oversight.

Responsible AI designs for human agency, fairness, transparency, privacy, safety and accountability across the lifecycle rather than applying an ethics checklist at the end.

Human oversight design specifies what people can see, decide, contest, override and repair. A nominal approval button is not meaningful oversight if the person lacks time or information.

<EngineeringMode>
Responsible AI changes architecture, product design and governance. Engineers must identify affected groups, failure severity, data sensitivity and accountability paths. Design must support transparency, appeal and human intervention where needed. Build work includes policy checks, evals and audit trails. Operations handle incidents and monitor disparate impact. The risk is treating responsibility as documentation rather than constraints that shape what gets built.
</EngineeringMode>

<RealExample>
The COMPAS recidivism-risk controversy is a widely cited responsible-AI case. Even when a scoring tool appears operationally useful, stakeholders questioned bias, transparency, contestability and institutional accountability. The lesson is that model performance cannot be separated from who is affected and how decisions are made.
</RealExample>
